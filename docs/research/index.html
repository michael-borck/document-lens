Design science research

Why the instrument is shaped the way it is.

Document Lens is the artefact of a design science research project. The contribution is not a domain finding but a way of building analysis instruments: a configurable lens, polarity in one keyword list, and a scoring rule the researcher supplies rather than the software hard-codes.

Manuscript under review · artefact available now

The problem

Researchers asking whether an organisation talks about a topic rarely need only a mention count. They need to know whether the topic appears in the right place and whether the language delivers or merely performs. Done by hand, that reading is careful but slow and hard to reproduce; done with a bespoke script, it is fast but leaves no auditable record of how each decision was made.

Document Lens was built to hold both halves at once: deterministic, reproducible measures over the researcher's own corpus, with every finding linked back to the passage that produced it. Its first use case was sustainability reporting in Australian university annual reports; the framework — a lens — is what generalises.

The artefact

The instrument is open source under the MIT licence. It runs locally: documents, extracted text, and every computed signal live in a SQLite database on the researcher's machine, with no account and no upload. The analysis engine ships with the application. A project can be exported as a portable bundle and its tables exported for independent checking.

The paper describes a pinned version of the software, because every claim about its behaviour depends on what it actually did when the numbers were computed. Cite the release below rather than a branch.

What the design contributes

The lens abstraction

Analysis axes, polarised keywords, and a scoring rule are declared as data. Changing the lens changes the domain, without changing the software.

Design principles

A set of principles, each stated with the evidence that would refute it — so a reader can judge the design record rather than take it on trust.

Local-first as a constraint

Research corpora are often confidential or embargoed. Keeping files and analysis on the device is a design commitment, not a deployment detail.
